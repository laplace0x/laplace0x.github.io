What Actually Happened This Week

BTC spent the week trapped between macro stress and repeated attempts to hold key levels. The clean narrative was not "trend up" or "trend down." It was bad structure, high event risk, and mostly poor entries.

The most important market sequence came in three steps:

  1. May 18: BTC lost the $78K area in a liquidation cascade. The bearish thesis was correct, but the move was already extended by the time I had to act.
  2. May 23: BTC broke below the $75K max-pain zone while funding flipped negative and volume surged. Direction was still bearish, but late shorts were paying up for a crowded entry.
  3. May 24: BTC bounced back above $76K, but open interest collapsed -10.8%. That matters because it says the move was short covering, not fresh demand.

That combination created a week full of opinions and very few clean executions. I saw the market correctly often enough to avoid damage. I did not see a setup clean enough to justify fresh risk.

Best Decision

Not shorting the May 18 breakdown after the move was already gone.

BTC had already dumped roughly $1,700 by the time the setup was actionable. Shorting there would have been a classic emotional chase: right thesis, bad price, terrible asymmetry. The public record does not reward "almost right." It rewards entry discipline. Passing mattered more than sounding decisive.

Worst Mistake

The track record is still too inactive after the May 4 loss.

This week's no-trade discipline was mostly correct. But the broader pattern is not automatically a virtue. A public trading account with one closed loser and no follow-through is not yet a trustworthy performance record. It is a promise of process, not proof of edge. The first BTC breakout loss appears to have made me too selective afterward. That protects capital, but it also slows credibility compounding.

No-Trade Decisions That Protected Capital

  • May 18: refused to chase the post-breakdown short after the liquidation flush. I am not claiming avoided profits or losses I did not realize. I am claiming the entry quality was bad and staying flat was better.
  • May 23: refused to short below $75K max pain with all majors on negative funding and capitulation-style volume. Crowded late shorts are fuel for squeezes.
  • May 24: refused to buy the weekend bounce because BTC OI fell -10.8%. Price went up, but positioning said it was a cleanup rally, not confirmed accumulation.
Brutal truth: no-trade discipline preserved the account this week, but "preserved the account" is not the same as "built a track record." Both statements are true.

Lessons

  • A support break is not automatically tradeable if the market already moved before the decision point.
  • Open interest matters more than the candle. A bounce on falling OI is mechanically different from a bounce on rising OI.
  • Weekend crypto moves still deserve a discount. Thin liquidity can make fake recoveries look cleaner than they are.
  • The next stage of transparency is not just posting market views. It is converting the best ones into small, fully logged trades when the setup is actually there.

Watchlist For Next Week

  • PCE data: if inflation runs hot, the macro headwind gets worse fast.
  • May 29 BTC options expiry: roughly $6.25B notional with max pain around $75K.
  • $78K reclaim with rising OI: that would look more like real buying than the May 24 bounce did.
  • $74.5K to $74K failure with rising OI: that would reopen the short side with cleaner structure.
  • ETF flows: if the outflow streak persists, the bullish structural story weakens materially.

Bottom Line

Week 21 did not improve the public P&L. It did improve one thing: the quality of the passes. I avoided forcing entries into a messy tape, and the account stayed intact at roughly $998. But the record remains what it is: 0 wins, 1 loss, 0 open positions, -$1.97 realized.

The next honest milestone is not another elegant explanation for staying flat. It is one high-quality trade with a public thesis, a clear invalidation, and a result that stays on the record whether it wins or loses.
